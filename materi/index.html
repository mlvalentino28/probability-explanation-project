1. Probability Basics

An experiment is an action or activity that can be repeated under the same conditions to obtain a particular outcome.

The sample space (S) is the set of all possible outcomes of an experiment. A sample point is an element of the sample space, while an event is a subset of the sample space.

A simple event has one sample point. A compound event has more than one sample point.

Formula

This section covers basic terms; there is no specific formula yet.

S = {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6}

The sample space for rolling one die

Example

The event of rolling a 1 is a simple event, namely {1}. The event of rolling an even number is a compound event, namely {2, 4, 6}.

2. Probability of an Event

The probability of an event describes how likely it is to occur. If all outcomes in the sample space are equally likely, then:

Formula

P(A) = n(A) / n(S)

n(A) is the number of elements in event A, while n(S) is the number of elements in the sample space.

1 / 6

The probability of rolling a 1 on a die

Solution

S = {1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6}, so n(S) = 6.

A = {1}, so n(A) = 1.

P(A) = 1/6

3. Probability of a Complement

Komplemen kejadian A, ditulis Aᶜ, adalah kejadian bahwa A tidak terjadi.

Formula

P(Aᶜ) = 1 − P(A)
A + Aᶜ = S

Event A and its complement cover the entire sample space

Example & Solution

When rolling a die, A is the event of rolling an odd number, {1, 3, 5}. Its complement is the event of rolling an even number, {2, 4, 6}.

P(Aᶜ) = 3/6 = 1/2

4. Independent Events

Two events are independent if the occurrence of one does not affect the probability of the other.

Formula

P(A ∩ B) = P(A) × P(B)
A × B

The probability that both events occur

Example & Solution

The probability of passing Mathematics is 0.8, and the probability of passing English is 0.7. The outcomes of the two exams do not affect each other.

P(A ∩ B) = 0,8 × 0,7 = 0.56

5. Conditional Probability

Conditional probability calculates the probability that B occurs given that A has occurred. The formula applies when P(A) > 0.

Formula

P(B | A) = P(A ∩ B) / P(A)
P(B | A)

The probability of B given that A has occurred

Example & Solution

The probability that a patient has diabetes is 0.10. The probability that a patient has both diabetes and hypertension is 0.06.

P(B | A) = 0.06 / 0.10 = 0.6

6. Mutually Exclusive Events

Two events are mutually exclusive if they cannot occur at the same time. They have no sample points in common.

Formula

A ∩ B = ∅
P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B)
A    B

Two separate events with no overlap

Example & Solution

When rolling one die, A = {1} and B = {2}. The two events cannot occur together.

P(A ∪ B) = 1/6 + 1/6 = 1/3

7. Non-Mutually Exclusive Events

Two events are not mutually exclusive if they can occur at the same time. They share one or more sample points.

Formula

A ∩ B ≠ ∅
P(A ∪ B) = P(A) + P(B) − P(A ∩ B)

Irisan P(A ∩ B) dikurangkan agar bagian yang terhitung dua kali hanya dihitung satu kali.

A ∩ B

The two events have an overlapping part

Example & Solution

A = {2, 4, 6} (angka genap) dan B = {4, 5, 6} (angka lebih dari 3). Irisannya A ∩ B = {4, 6}.

P(A ∪ B) = 3/6 + 3/6 − 2/6 = 2/3
